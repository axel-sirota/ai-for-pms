1.3 The Model Landscape

Just enough to have informed conversations — model choice matters less than you think

The PM Insight That Changes Everything

Model choice matters less than system design. 80% of value comes from product decisions — how you frame the problem, design the UX, handle errors, and evaluate quality. Spending weeks debating GPT-4 vs Claude is usually wasted time.

PM Decision: Which Tier?

Most products should start with mid-tier models (GPT-4o-mini, Claude Haiku). They're 10-50x cheaper and "good enough" for 80% of use cases. Upgrade to frontier models only when mid-tier measurably fails on your specific task.

The pattern: Prototype with frontier → measure quality → test with mid-tier → if quality holds, ship mid-tier and save 90% on costs.

What Actually Matters (More Than Model Choice)

1. Prompt Design

A well-crafted prompt on a mid-tier model often beats a lazy prompt on a frontier model. Your prompts are your product.

2. System Architecture

RAG, caching, routing between models, fallback chains — these decisions have 10x more impact than model selection.

3. Evaluation

If you can't measure quality, you can't improve it. Invest in evals before investing in model upgrades.

4. Guardrails

The fastest model in the world is useless if it leaks PII or hallucinates to customers. Safety is non-negotiable.

Questions to Ask Your Engineering Team

Before Choosing a Model, Ask:
  1. What's our latency requirement? (Real-time chat vs batch processing changes everything)
  2. What's our budget per query? ($0.001 vs $0.10 per query is a 100x difference at scale)
  3. Do we need on-premise? (Eliminates cloud-only providers)
  4. What's our data sensitivity level? (Some data can't leave your infrastructure)
  5. Can we swap models later? (Avoid vendor lock-in; abstract the LLM layer)

Stakeholder Framing

When your CTO asks "Which model should we use?", here's your answer:

"The model is the least important decision we'll make. What matters more is how we design the prompts, architect the system, and evaluate quality. I'd recommend we start with a mid-tier model, measure performance on our specific use case, and only upgrade if we have data showing we need to. That approach saves us 90% on costs and lets us ship faster."
