2.3 Why Models Fail

Three failure modes every PM must recognize — no formulas required

Why This Matters for PMs

You don't need to diagnose these problems yourself — that's your data scientist's job. But you need to recognize the symptoms so you can ask the right questions and avoid shipping a broken model.

1. Overfitting: The Model Memorized, It Didn't Learn

Good Fit vs Overfitting 
  Good Model:                    Overfitted Model:
  Finds the TREND               Memorizes EVERY point

     *    *                         *    *
   *   **    *                    */  \*/   \*
  *  *    *    *                 *  *    *    *
       smooth curve                  wiggly mess
       (generalizes)                 (memorized noise)

What's happening?

The model learned the training data too well — including the noise and random patterns. It performs perfectly on historical data but fails on new data.

PM Signal

Red Flag

"Our model is 99% accurate on test data but only 70% in production."

Questions to Ask

  • "Are we testing on data the model has never seen?"
  • "How different is our test data from production data?"
  • "Is the model simpler than the data requires, or too complex?"

2. Underfitting: The Model Is Too Simple

Underfitting 
  The data has a curve:           The model draws a line:

     *    *                          *    *
   *   **    *                     ----*-------*--
  *  *    *    *                  *  *    *    *
       curved pattern                 straight line
                                      (misses the pattern)

What's happening?

The model is too simple to capture the real patterns in the data. Like trying to describe a mountain range with a flat line.

PM Signal

Red Flag

"We've been training for weeks but accuracy won't improve beyond 60%, even on training data."

Questions to Ask

  • "Do we have enough features (input variables) for this task?"
  • "Is the model type appropriate for this problem?"
  • "Is there actually a predictable pattern in this data?"

3. Data Drift: The World Changed, the Model Didn't

Real Example: COVID & Demand Forecasting

Every demand forecasting model trained on pre-2020 data broke when COVID hit. The patterns the models learned — seasonal shopping, commute-based purchases, office supply orders — simply stopped being true.

What's happening?

The data distribution in production is different from what the model was trained on. The model's "rules" no longer apply because the world has changed.

Three Types of Drift

TypeWhat It Looks LikeExample
Gradual Performance slowly degrades over months Customer preferences shift over time
Sudden Performance drops sharply Pandemic, regulatory change, competitor launch
Seasonal Performance varies predictably by season Holiday shopping vs normal periods

PM Signal

Red Flag

"The model was great 6 months ago but accuracy has dropped to 65%. Nothing changed in our code."

Questions to Ask

  • "When was the model last retrained?"
  • "Do we have monitoring for performance drift?"
  • "What's our retraining schedule and trigger?"

The PM Cheat Sheet

Failure ModePM SignalFirst Question to Ask
Overfitting Great on test data, bad in production "How different is test data from production?"
Underfitting Accuracy never improves, even on training data "Do we have enough signal in this data?"
Data Drift Performance degrades over time "When was the model last retrained?"

Stakeholder Framing

When someone asks "Why did the model stop working?":

"ML models aren't like software — they don't stay accurate forever. They learn patterns from historical data, and when the world changes, those patterns become outdated. We need to monitor performance continuously and retrain every 3-6 months, just like we update our forecasts."
