2.5 Cost of Errors in Dollars

Turn model tradeoffs into the language stakeholders understand: money

The Framework That Changes Conversations

When you can say "Improving recall from 85% to 95% saves us $2.3M per year in fraud losses, but increases false alarms by $180K" — you're speaking a language every executive understands. This framework turns abstract model metrics into concrete dollars.

The Cost Framework

Expected Monthly Cost of Errors

Total Cost = (Number of False Positives × Cost per FP) + (Number of False Negatives × Cost per FN)

That's it. No complex math. Just count the errors and multiply by what each one costs your business.

Worked Example: Fraud Detection

Scenario: Your fraud detection model processes 100,000 transactions/month. Actual fraud rate is 1% (1,000 fraudulent transactions).

MetricModel A (High Recall)Model B (High Precision)
Recall95% (catches 950 of 1,000 frauds)80% (catches 800 of 1,000 frauds)
Precision70% (30% of flags are false alarms)95% (only 5% are false alarms)
False Positives407 legit flagged42 legit flagged
False Negatives50 frauds missed200 frauds missed

Costs: FP = $50 (customer friction) | FN = $5,000 (fraud loss)

Model A (High Recall)Model B (High Precision)
FP Cost407 × $50 = $20,35042 × $50 = $2,100
FN Cost50 × $5,000 = $250,000200 × $5,000 = $1,000,000
Total Monthly Cost$270,350$1,002,100
The Decision

Model A costs $732K/month less than Model B, despite having more false alarms. When false negatives cost 100x more than false positives, recall wins. This is the conversation to have with your stakeholders.

I recommend Model A. The $20K in extra customer friction is a bargain compared to $750K in prevented fraud losses."
