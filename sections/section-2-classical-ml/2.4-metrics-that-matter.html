2.4 Metrics That Matter

Precision, recall, and F1 - reframed as business decisions

The Question That Changes Everything

A fire detection model that always predicts "no fire" is 99.9% accurate. (Because fires are rare - 0.1% of the time.)

Is that a good model?

Of course not. It would miss every fire. Accuracy alone is meaningless. You need metrics that match your business cost of being wrong.

The Confusion Matrix (No Math, Just Logic)

Every prediction falls into one of four buckets. Think of a fraud detection system:

What the Model Predicted
TPTrue Positive
"Correctly caught fraud"
FPFalse Positive
"Flagged a legit transaction"
FNFalse Negative
"Missed real fraud"
TNTrue Negative
"Correctly approved legit"

Top row = model said "fraud" | Bottom row = model said "not fraud"
Left column = actually fraud | Right column = actually not fraud

Three Metrics, Three Business Questions

Metric The Business Question Prioritize When... Example
Precision "When we flag something, how often are we right?" False positives are expensive Spam filter: Don't send real emails to spam
Recall "Of all the real cases, how many did we catch?" Missing cases is dangerous Fraud detection: Catch every fraud, even if some legit transactions get flagged
F1 Score "What's the balance between precision and recall?" Both types of errors matter equally Content moderation: Don't miss harmful content, but don't over-censor
The Only Question You Need to Ask

"What does a false positive cost us vs a false negative?"

If false positives are more expensive → optimize for precision.
If false negatives are more expensive → optimize for recall.
If both matter equally → optimize for F1.

Business Scenarios: Which Metric Wins?

Fraud Detection

FP cost: $50 (customer friction, support call)
FN cost: $5,000 (average fraud loss)

Optimize for RECALL

Better to flag some legit transactions than miss real fraud.

Email Spam Filter

FP cost: $1,000+ (missed contract, lost deal)
FN cost: $0.10 (spam in inbox, minor annoyance)

Optimize for PRECISION

Never send real emails to spam. Let some spam through instead.

Cancer Screening

FP cost: $500 (unnecessary biopsy, anxiety)
FN cost: Life-threatening (missed cancer)

Optimize for RECALL (strongly)

Missing cancer is unacceptable. Accept more false alarms.

Credit Approval

FP cost: $500 (lost revenue from rejected good customer)
FN cost: $5,000 (bad debt from approved defaulter)

Optimize for RECALL (moderate)

Lean toward catching defaults, but don't reject too many good customers.

Stakeholder Framing

When someone asks "What's the accuracy of our model?":

"Accuracy alone doesn't tell us enough. The real question is: what's the cost of each type of error? For our fraud model, missing a fraud case costs us $5,000, but a false alarm only costs $50. So we optimize for catching every fraud case, even if it means a few false alarms. That's recall, and ours is at 94%."
