2.1 When Classical ML Wins

Structured data + prediction = Classical ML's sweet spot

Failure Story: Knight Capital

In August 2012, Knight Capital deployed a trading algorithm update. A configuration error caused the system to execute millions of unintended trades in 45 minutes, resulting in a $440 million loss — enough to bankrupt the company.

The ML model wasn't wrong. The system around it — deployment, monitoring, kill switches — failed. Knight Capital was acquired at a fire-sale price within months.

PM Decision Point

As the PM, you would have needed to ask:

  • What's the maximum loss before we automatically halt trading?
  • How do we test a deployment before it touches real money?
  • Who monitors the system in the first hour after deployment?
  • What's the rollback procedure if something goes wrong?

Lesson: The ML model is the easy part. The product decisions around it determine success or catastrophe.

Classical ML Wins When You Have...

Structured Data

Rows and columns. Spreadsheets. Database tables. Things with numbers, dates, categories.

Transaction amounts, login frequency, purchase history, demographics

A Prediction Task

You want to predict a number (regression) or a category (classification) from historical patterns.

"Will this customer churn?" "How much will this cost?" "Is this fraud?"

Labeled Examples

Thousands of historical examples where you know the answer. The model learns patterns from these.

Past transactions labeled fraud/not-fraud, past customers labeled churned/retained

Explainability Needs

Regulators, compliance, or stakeholders need to understand why the model made a decision.

"Why was this loan denied?" needs an answer, not a black box

Why Not Just Use an LLM?

Classical ML vs LLM for Structured Data
FactorClassical MLLLM
Accuracy on tabular dataHigher (purpose-built)Lower (not designed for this)
Inference speedMillisecondsSeconds
Cost per 1,000 predictions~$0.001~$1-10
ExplainabilityFeature importance built-inBlack box
Setup timeWeeks-months (need data pipeline)Hours (just a prompt)

Rule of thumb: If your data fits in a spreadsheet and you're predicting a number or category, Classical ML wins on accuracy, cost, and speed. LLMs win on flexibility and speed-to-prototype.

Stakeholder Framing

When engineering says "We want to use a large language model for fraud detection":

"For structured data predictions like fraud detection, Classical ML is 1,000x cheaper per prediction, runs in milliseconds instead of seconds, and provides explainability that compliance requires. LLMs are powerful for text tasks, but this isn't a text task — it's a pattern-matching task on transaction data. Let's use the right tool for the job."
