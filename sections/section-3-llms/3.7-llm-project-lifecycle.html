Section 3 - LLMOps

The LLM Project Lifecycle

How AI projects work differently - faster iteration, new metrics, and your role at each phase.

Building on Section 2.2

In Section 2, you learned the ML Lifecycle: 60% data work, months of model training, deterministic metrics. The LLM project lifecycle (LLMOps) is fundamentally different - faster iteration, no training data work, and new challenges like prompt engineering and non-deterministic output.

Failure Story: The Hallucinating Launch

A B2B SaaS company rushed their AI assistant to market in 6 weeks. No systematic evaluation. No monitoring. On day 3, a customer screenshot went viral: the assistant had confidently recommended a competitor's product as "superior."

They pulled the feature, spent 8 weeks rebuilding with proper evaluation, and lost a key enterprise deal.

Lesson: LLM projects move fast, but skipping evaluation phases creates expensive failures.

MLOps vs LLMOps: Key Differences

Aspect MLOps (Section 2.2) LLMOps (This Module)
Focus Training, validation, deployment Prompt management, inference optimization
Data Work 60% of time - collecting, labeling, cleaning 0-10% (unless fine-tuning)
Iteration Speed Weeks to months Hours to days
Experimentation Hyperparameter tuning Prompt refinement, model routing
Metrics Accuracy, precision, recall Relevance, toxicity, hallucination rate
Monitoring Model drift, accuracy degradation Output quality, cost per query, latency
The Big Difference

In ML, you spend months on data before you have anything to show. In LLMs, you can have a working prototype in hours - but production quality requires systematic evaluation that most teams skip.

1. Problem Definition

What happens: Define the business problem, success criteria, and whether AI is the right solution.

PM role:

  • Define "what good looks like" in business terms
  • Identify failure modes that matter (hallucination? latency? cost?)
  • Decide: prompting, RAG, or fine-tuning?

Duration: 1-2 weeks

2. Data Investigation

What happens: Identify what data the LLM needs access to. For RAG: what documents? For fine-tuning: what examples?

PM role:

  • Approve data sources (privacy, freshness, bias)
  • Define what content is in-scope vs out-of-scope
  • Identify gaps in existing documentation

Duration: 1-3 weeks

3. Development (Prompting)

What happens: Prompt engineering, RAG pipeline setup, integration with your product.

PM role:

  • Review system prompt constraints
  • Provide edge case scenarios to test
  • Define output format requirements

Duration: 2-6 weeks

4. Evaluation

What happens: Systematic testing against real scenarios. Human review of outputs. Red-team testing.

PM role:

  • Define test scenarios from customer use cases
  • Review sample outputs for quality
  • Decide go/no-go based on eval metrics

Duration: 1-3 weeks (don't skip this!)

5. Deployment

What happens: Production rollout with monitoring, guardrails, and fallback systems.

PM role:

  • Define rollout strategy (canary, A/B, full)
  • Set up cost alerts and rate limits
  • Prepare incident response plan

Duration: 1-2 weeks

6. Monitor & Iterate

What happens: Continuous monitoring, user feedback collection, prompt refinement.

PM role:

  • Review quality dashboards weekly
  • Triage user complaints about AI responses
  • Prioritize prompt improvements

Duration: Ongoing

Common Timeline Mistakes

What Teams Say Reality What to Do
"We'll have it working in a week" A demo, yes. Production-quality? 6-10 weeks minimum. Separate "demo" timeline from "production" timeline
"We don't need evaluation - it's just prompting" Prompting is easy. Systematic quality is hard. Budget 1-3 weeks for eval. It saves months later.
"We'll fix issues after launch" One viral bad response costs more than proper eval. Soft-launch to internal users first. Always.
"The model is good enough" Good enough for demos != good enough for production. Define quantitative success criteria before building

Questions to Ask at Each Phase

Before Starting

  • "Is AI actually the right solution here?"
  • "What does 'good enough' look like?"
  • "What's the cost of getting it wrong?"
  • "Prompting, RAG, or fine-tuning?"

During Development

  • "Have we tested the edge cases I identified?"
  • "What's our hallucination rate on the test set?"
  • "Is the system prompt defensive enough?"
  • "What happens when the model doesn't know?"

Before Launch

  • "Have we done red-team testing?"
  • "What's the rollback plan?"
  • "Do we have cost alerts set up?"
  • "Who's on call for the first week?"

Post-Launch

  • "What's our cost per query?"
  • "What failure modes are most common?"
  • "What are users asking that we don't handle?"
  • "Is quality stable or degrading?"

Quick Check

Your team has a working LLM feature demo after 2 weeks. The VP wants to launch next week. What do you do?

Stakeholder Framing

When your VP says "Why does this AI feature take 8 weeks? I thought LLMs were fast?":

"You're right that we can have a working demo in days - that's the power of LLMs. But a demo that works in the conference room is different from a feature that works for 10,000 customers. We need 2-3 weeks for systematic evaluation to catch hallucinations, edge cases, and prompt injection attacks before they become viral screenshots. That investment upfront prevents the 8-week fire drill of pulling a broken feature."
