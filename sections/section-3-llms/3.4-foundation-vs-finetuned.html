Foundation vs Fine-tuned

When to prompt, when to fine-tune, and why 90% of the time prompting wins.

The Expensive Mistake

A fintech startup spent 3 months and $200K fine-tuning GPT-3.5 on their customer support data. The result was 2% better than a well-crafted system prompt that took one engineer a week.

The fine-tuned model also couldn't be updated when policies changed without another round of training.

Lesson: Fine-tuning is a last resort, not a first instinct.

The Three Approaches

Approach What It Is Cost Time Data Needed When to Use
Prompting Use a foundation model with well-crafted instructions $0 (just API costs) Hours to days None First approach. Always try this first.
Fine-tuning Retrain an existing model on your specific data $500–$50K Weeks 1,000+ examples Prompting fails AND you have good data
Training from scratch Build a model from zero $1M–$100M+ Months to years Billions of tokens Almost never. Only for large AI companies.

The 90% Rule

90% of the time, better prompts beat fine-tuning.

Before spending money on fine-tuning, try: more specific system prompts, few-shot examples in the prompt, chain-of-thought reasoning, or RAG (grounding the model with your data).

3 Questions Before Fine-tuning

1. Have you exhausted prompting?

Have you tried: persona prompts, few-shot examples, chain-of-thought, output format constraints, and RAG? If not, start there.

2. Do you have clean, labeled data?

Fine-tuning needs 1,000+ examples of input-output pairs. Bad data = bad model. Budget 60% of fine-tuning time for data prep.

3. Can you maintain it?

Fine-tuned models need retraining when your product changes. Budget for ongoing maintenance, not just initial training.

Real-World Comparison

Scenario Best Approach Why
Customer support chatbot Prompting + RAG Policies change often. RAG keeps responses current without retraining.
Medical note summarizer Fine-tuning Domain-specific language, high accuracy required, stable format.
Email categorizer Prompting Simple classification. A good system prompt with examples handles this easily.
Legal contract analyzer Fine-tuning + RAG Domain expertise needed, but also needs access to specific case law.
Marketing copy writer Prompting Brand voice can be captured in a system prompt. Fine-tuning overkill.

Quick Check

Your team wants to fine-tune a model for your customer support bot. They've collected 200 example conversations. What do you do?

Stakeholder Framing

When engineering says "We need to fine-tune the model for better results":

"Before we invest in fine-tuning, let's validate that we've exhausted prompting approaches. Have we tried few-shot examples, chain-of-thought, and RAG? Fine-tuning costs $X and takes Y weeks, plus ongoing maintenance. If prompting gets us to 90% quality, that last 10% may not justify the investment."
