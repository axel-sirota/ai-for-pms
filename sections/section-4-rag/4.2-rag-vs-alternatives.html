Section 4 - RAG

RAG vs Alternatives

RAG, fine-tuning, or long context? The PM decision tree for grounding AI in company knowledge.

Failure Story: The $200K Fine-Tuning Mistake

A fintech startup spent 3 months and $200K fine-tuning GPT-3.5 on their customer support data. The result was 2% better than a well-crafted system prompt that took one engineer a week.

Worse: when their policies changed, the fine-tuned model kept giving outdated answers. They had to retrain - another $50K and 3 weeks.

Lesson: Fine-tuning is a last resort, not a first instinct. RAG handles updates without retraining.

PM Decision Point

Your engineering team proposes grounding the AI in company knowledge. You need to choose:

  • RAG - Retrieve docs at query time
  • Fine-tuning - Retrain the model on your data
  • Long Context - Stuff all docs in the prompt

The right choice depends on update frequency, corpus size, and citation needs.

The Three Approaches

Approach How It Works Best For Watch Out
RAG Retrieve relevant docs at query time, add to prompt Frequently changing info, need citations, large corpus Chunking complexity, retrieval quality
Fine-tuning Retrain model weights on your data Stable domain knowledge, specific style, high volume Expensive, slow updates, can't cite sources
Long Context Put all docs directly in prompt Small corpus (<100K tokens), simple use cases Cost scales with corpus, can't scale

Real-World Examples

Customer Support Bot

Best: RAG

10,000 support articles that update weekly. Customers need accurate answers with links to source articles.

Why not fine-tuning? Would need constant retraining. Can't provide citations.

Medical Note Summarizer

Best: Fine-tuning

Consistent format, domain-specific terminology, 50,000 notes/day. Format must be identical every time.

Why not RAG? No need to retrieve - just transform. Style consistency matters more than citations.

Internal HR Chatbot

Best: Long Context

15 HR policy documents (~50 pages total). Policies change quarterly.

Why not RAG? Corpus is small enough to fit in context. RAG adds unnecessary complexity.

Legal Contract Analyzer

Best: RAG

500+ contracts, 1000+ case summaries. Must cite specific clauses and precedents.

Why not fine-tuning? Need citations for legal compliance. Corpus too large for context.

Stakeholder Framing

When engineering says "We should fine-tune the model for better results":

"Before we invest in fine-tuning, let's validate the requirements. Fine-tuning costs $50K-$200K and takes weeks, plus we'd need to retrain whenever our docs change. RAG costs $1K-$10K, updates in minutes, and gives us citations. Unless we have a specific style or format requirement that RAG can't handle, RAG is the safer bet."
