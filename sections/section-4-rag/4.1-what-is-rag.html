Section 4 - RAG

What Is RAG?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation: grounding AI in your documents instead of its imagination.

Failure Story: The Lawyer's $5,000 Mistake

In 2023, attorneys at a New York law firm used ChatGPT to research case precedents. They submitted a court brief citing 6 cases that didn't exist - the LLM hallucinated them completely, with fake case names, fake citations, and fake rulings.

The judge was not amused. The lawyers were fined $5,000 and publicly sanctioned.

Lesson: An LLM without grounding in real documents will confidently make up facts. RAG solves this - but only if implemented correctly.

PM Decision Point

When your team proposes an LLM feature that needs to answer questions about company data, you have three options:

  • Hope the LLM knows it - It doesn't. Or worse, it thinks it does.
  • Fine-tune the model - Expensive, slow, can't update easily.
  • Use RAG - Retrieve real docs, feed to LLM, get grounded answers.

RAG is usually the right answer. Here's why.

RAG in Plain English

1. Retrieval

When a user asks a question, find the most relevant chunks from your document corpus. This uses embeddings (from Section 3) to match by meaning, not keywords.

2. Augmentation

Take those retrieved chunks and add them to the prompt: "Here's context from our docs. Now answer the user's question based on this."

3. Generation

The LLM generates an answer grounded in the retrieved context. It can cite sources: "According to the Return Policy document..."

Why RAG Works

Problem Without RAG With RAG
Hallucination LLM invents facts from training data LLM answers from retrieved docs
Outdated info Training data is months/years old Docs can be updated anytime
Company-specific LLM doesn't know your policies Your docs are the source of truth
Citations Can't prove where answer came from Can cite exact source documents

The Catch: RAG Creates New Problems

RAG Isn't Magic

RAG solves hallucination but introduces new failure modes:

  • Bad chunking - Important info split across chunks
  • Wrong retrieval - Similar words, wrong context
  • Ignored context - Model hallucinates despite having the right docs
  • Missing info - Question not covered in your docs

We'll cover each of these in Module 4.4.

Stakeholder Framing

When your CEO asks "Why can't the AI just know our policies?":

"The AI was trained on internet data - it doesn't have our specific policies. That's like hiring someone who read every Wikipedia article but never saw our employee handbook. RAG is how we give the AI access to our actual documents, so it answers from our source of truth instead of guessing."
