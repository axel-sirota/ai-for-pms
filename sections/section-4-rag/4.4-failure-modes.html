Section 4 - RAG

RAG Failure Modes

RAG isn't magic. Here are the 5 ways it fails - and how to spot them before your users do.

Failure Story: Bing Chat's Contradictory Citations

When Microsoft launched Bing Chat with RAG, users quickly found it would cite sources that directly contradicted its own answers.

Example: Bing would say "The movie released in 2019" while citing an article clearly stating "released in 2021."

The retrieval worked - it found the right source. But the generation ignored the context.

Lesson: RAG can fail even when retrieval succeeds. You need to monitor the full pipeline.

PM Decision Point

When your RAG system gives wrong answers, you need to diagnose where it failed:

  • Did retrieval find the wrong chunks? (retrieval failure)
  • Did retrieval find the right chunks but they were incomplete? (chunking failure)
  • Did the model ignore the retrieved context? (generation failure)

Different failures need different fixes.

1. Bad Chunking

What happens: Important info is split across chunks. Retrieval finds a chunk, but it's incomplete.

Example: "The price is $99 per" in one chunk, "month with unlimited users" in another.

How to spot: Answers are partially correct but missing key details.

Fix: Sentence-aware chunking, increase overlap, test with real queries.

2. Wrong Retrieval

What happens: Semantic similarity matches keywords but misses context. Retrieved chunks are related but don't answer the question.

Example: User asks about "software refund policy." Retrieves "software licensing" and "general refunds" - neither covers software-specific refunds.

How to spot: Answers are confident but off-topic.

Fix: Hybrid search (keywords + embeddings), metadata filtering, better chunking.

3. Hallucination Despite Context

What happens: The model ignores retrieved context and makes up an answer anyway.

Example: Retrieved chunk says "30-day returns." Model says "60-day returns."

How to spot: Answers contradict the cited sources.

Fix: Stronger grounding prompts, better models, output validation against sources.

4. Outdated Info Conflict

What happens: Your docs say X, but the model's training data says Y. Model prefers its "knowledge."

Example: Your doc says "Free shipping over $50." Model says "$35" (old policy in training data).

How to spot: Answers match old info that you've since updated.

Fix: Explicit prompting to prioritize retrieved context, use newer models with less domain knowledge.

5. Missing Info Hallucination

What happens: The question isn't covered in your docs. Instead of saying "I don't know," the model invents an answer.

Example: User asks about student discounts. No docs mention it. Model says "Yes, 15% with valid ID."

How to spot: Answers to questions your docs don't cover.

Fix: Low-confidence detection, explicit "I don't know" training, retrieval confidence thresholds.
